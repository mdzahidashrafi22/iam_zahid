# Project 3 · 01 — Business Context

## The Indian FMCG Industry

| Indicator | FY24 value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Industry size | ~$220 Bn (₹18.3 lakh Cr) | Nielsen / IBEF |
| Growth rate FY21-24 CAGR | ~9% (volume), ~14% (value) | Industry data |
| Rural share | ~37% | Nielsen |
| Modern trade penetration | ~13% (mostly urban) | Industry |
| GST | 18% on most processed foods, 12% on dairy | GST Council |

**Key economic features:**

1. **Distribution depth is the moat.** Reaching 8-9 Mn outlets in India is a 30-year capex; new entrants can't replicate.
2. **Working capital cycles are short.** Distributors pay in 7-21 days. Suppliers paid in 30-60 days. Negative working capital is normal.
3. **Brand premium = pricing power = margin durability.** A premium brand at full distribution can hold prices for 18-24 months in inflation.
4. **Rural demand is cyclical** (monsoon, agri-income). Urban demand is more stable.
5. **Input cost volatility** — palm oil, milk solids, wheat, packaging — drives quarterly margin volatility but generally pass-through-able over 6-9 months.

## Why These Three for Comparison

- **Same broad industry** (FMCG) — comparison fair
- **Different positioning**:
  - Nestlé = premium foods, urban-centric
  - Britannia = mass biscuits + dairy, urban + tier-2/3
  - HUL = mass everything, deepest rural
- **Same accounting framework** (Ind AS) — ratios comparable
- **Public, audited, four-year clean data** — reproducible

## What "Real Data" Means Here

Unlike Projects 1 & 2 (anchored synthetic), Project 3 uses **only publicly-filed numbers**:

- Audited annual reports (PDF) for FY21, FY22, FY23, FY24
- Investor presentations (PDF) for segment & operating commentary
- Stock exchange filings (BSE/NSE) for quarterly numbers
- screener.in for ratio cross-checks (independent source for triangulation)
- Bloomberg / Reuters for share price (if relevant)

Every number in the workbook traces to a specific page of a specific document. This is the **same standard a sell-side equity analyst would use** — and is the level a recruiter at a brokerage / mutual fund / private-equity firm expects.

## The Investment Question

If you held only one of these three for 5 years, which would you choose, and why?

This is the question driving the analysis. It forces every ratio, every chart, into service of a defended view.

## Notes on FY Conventions

This is a real-data project so FY conventions matter:

- **Britannia & HUL:** April–March (Indian standard)
- **Nestlé India:** January–December (calendar year, follows Swiss parent)
- For comparison, I align Nestlé CY23 data with Britannia/HUL FY24 (the closest 12-month overlap). This is documented and disclosed in [02-dataset-description.md](02-dataset-description.md). The alternative — using calendar quarters reconstructed for all three — adds noise without adding insight.

The willingness to address such conventions explicitly is itself a credibility signal in interviews.
