# Project 2 · 05 — Recommendations

The recommendations are split into:
- **A. FY24 lessons** — what we change immediately
- **B. FY25 plan design** — what the new budget includes
- **C. Governance** — process changes to prevent recurrence

---

## A. Immediate (Q1 FY25) Actions

### A1. Redesign Sales Commission Plan — tie to gross-margin contribution

| Element | Current FY24 plan | New FY25 plan |
|---|---|---|
| Base commission | 8% of ACV, all tiers | 8% × tier-multiplier (1.4× Enterprise / 1.0× Growth / 0.65× Starter) |
| Accelerator | 12% above 110% quota | Unchanged but quota is now GM-contribution, not ACV |
| Ramp commission (first 6 mo) | Same as full | 50% of full + bonus for first Enterprise win |
| Clawback | Logo churn within 6 mo | + Logo churn within 12 mo (Starter only) — discourages bad-fit deals |

**Expected behavioural impact (modelled):** Enterprise share of pipeline returns to 22-24% by Q3 FY25. Net commission spend stays flat; **mix-quality gain ≈ ₹1.4 Cr in EBITDA terms**.

**Risk:** rep dissatisfaction & attrition. Mitigated by:
- Grandfathering existing pipeline at old plan
- Q1 transparent Q&A sessions with Sales team
- Top-rep re-targeting (highest performers can earn 18-22% more under new plan if they hold mix)

### A2. Pause SDR hiring until existing cohort productivity is established

| Action | Detail |
|---|---|
| Pause | All new SDR/AE hiring through Q2 FY25 |
| Re-evaluate | Q3 FY25 against ramp-cohort productivity scorecard |
| Trigger to resume | When Q1-FY24 cohort hits 70% productive on Enterprise opportunities |

This is counter-cultural for a Series-B (growth-mode) but the **CAC-payback math justifies it.** Hiring more ramp reps now adds CAC without adding productive Enterprise capacity for 12+ months.

### A3. Reclass POSH feature spend ₹0.18 Cr from R&D to Sales-mandated bucket

Procedural — no money moves, but FY24 Sales accountability bridge becomes:
- Volume + Price + Mix variance: ₹2.59 Cr
- Sales-mandated R&D spend: ₹0.18 Cr
- **Total Sales accountability: ₹2.77 Cr** (vs ₹2.59 Cr before reclass)

This is **an honest accounting** that doesn't change reality but improves attribution.

## B. FY25 Budget Design Changes

### B1. Mix-locked revenue plan with mechanism

| Plan tier | FY25 plan share | Mechanism |
|---|---:|---|
| Enterprise | 26% | New compensation 1.4× multiplier + Q3-onwards Enterprise-only AE recruitment |
| Growth | 46% | Default for ramping reps post-Q1 ramp |
| Starter | 28% | Inbound-only (no SDR outbound); product-led growth motion |

The **mechanism column** is what distinguishes a credible plan from a hopeful one.

### B2. Variance early-warning tripwires

Monthly KPI dashboard now includes (with thresholds):

| Tripwire | Trigger |
|---|---|
| New-business mix Enterprise share | < 20% in any month → CFO alert |
| Pipeline coverage Enterprise | < 3.5× quarterly target → CRO + CFO meeting |
| CAC payback (rolling 90-day) | > 18 months → review |
| Hosting cost / customer | > +5% drift → CTO action |
| S&M flexed variance run-rate | > +6% of plan → CMO review |

These are **leading indicators** designed to give 60-90 days of warning before the next year's bridge has the same shape.

### B3. Quarterly commission-plan review with Finance

Standing item, scheduled at month 11 of each quarter, attendees: CRO, CFO, FP&A, HRBP. Output: a one-page memo on whether the commission plan is producing the desired mix; if not, what micro-adjustments to make for the next quarter.

This is the single most-important governance change. It is the answer to *"why didn't anyone catch this earlier?"* — the answer is "no one was looking; now they will."

### B4. R&D scope-change discipline

Any feature additions that exceed 10% of the function's quarterly capex/opex budget require:
1. Written CFO sign-off
2. Re-baselining of the function's variance tracker
3. Identification of cost owner (was it Product, Sales, or Customer-driven?)

## C. Governance / Process

### C1. Replace static budget with rolling forecast (quarterly)

The annual budget remains the contractual document with the Board. But **monthly variance reporting now uses the latest rolling forecast**, not the original budget. This avoids the year-end "shock" by surfacing drift earlier.

### C2. Three-statement-aware variance dashboard

Add **cash variance** and **billings variance** to the existing revenue/EBITDA variance view. SaaS economics demand all three; one view is incomplete.

### C3. Audit-committee variance review framework

Adopt the format used by listed Indian SaaS (Freshworks 8-K analogue): every quarter, a **5-page variance memo** distributed 7 days before the meeting:
- Page 1: Executive bridge
- Page 2: Revenue decomposition
- Page 3: Cost decomposition
- Page 4: Tripwires status
- Page 5: Forward-looking scenario

This is professional-grade governance — and demonstrates CMA institutional value to the board.

## What This Demonstrates About the CMA

Most "variance analyses" stop at *what happened*. A mature CMA delivers:

1. **What happened** — the bridge.
2. **Why it happened** — the causal chain (mix → ramp + commission).
3. **Why it wasn't caught** — governance gap (no quarterly commission review).
4. **What changes** — design choices, not platitudes.
5. **What the new number is** — the FY25 commitment with mechanism.

That five-step structure is what separates a "junior analyst" from a "future CFO" answer in interviews.
