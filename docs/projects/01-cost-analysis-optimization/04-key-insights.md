# Project 1 · 04 — Key Insights

> **The brief was: "explain the 460 bps margin loss and propose a recovery plan."**
> The data tells a story that is **not what management was assuming**.

---

## Insight 1 — The 460 bps decomposition

Most of the loss is **not** raw-material price.

| Driver of margin compression | bps lost | % of total | Controllable by us? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| RM price-inflation un-passed (20% gap) | 145 | 32% | Partly (re-negotiate LTC clause) |
| Scrap rate increase (2.6% → 3.4% blended) | 78 | 17% | **Yes — operations** |
| Energy cost / unit increase | 42 | 9% | Partly (open-access power) |
| Mix shift to lower-margin SKUs (Plant-B ramp) | 96 | 21% | **Yes — sales mix** |
| Wage inflation > volume growth | 51 | 11% | Partly |
| Logistics & freight | 28 | 6% | Partly |
| Other (forex, depreciation step-up) | 20 | 4% | No |
| **Total** | **460** | **100%** | |

> **The CFO's hypothesis ("RM price")** explained 32% of the gap.
> **The dominant explainable driver was scrap + mix (38% combined)** — entirely controllable.

This is the **single most important slide in the deck.** It re-routes management attention from procurement to operations.

## Insight 2 — Plant-B is structurally more expensive per unit, and it's not utilisation alone

Plant-B (Chennai) was started 8 years after Plant-A — newer machines, same labour rates. Yet:

| Metric | Plant-A (Pune) | Plant-B (Chennai) | Variance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Conversion cost / kg | ₹46 | ₹54 | +17% |
| Scrap rate | 2.4% | 4.7% | +2.3 ppt |
| OEE | 71% | 58% | −13 ppt |
| Energy / part (kWh) | 2.1 | 2.8 | +33% |
| Utilisation | 78% | 64% | −14 ppt |

If utilisation were the only issue, fixed cost / part would be high but variable rates would match. They don't — **Plant-B's variable rates (scrap, energy) are also worse**, meaning there's an operating-discipline gap that more volume alone won't fix. Recommendation #2 (scrap reduction) is therefore biased toward Plant-B.

## Insight 3 — The "mix shift" was self-inflicted

A consciously taken decision in FY23 to bring on a new aluminium-knuckle programme at Plant-B added ₹38 Cr of revenue but at 11% gross margin — vs the company average of 19%. **The programme is dilutive, but management approved it on a "fixed-cost absorption" argument.**

That argument doesn't hold up under contribution-margin analysis:

| | Reported logic | Contribution-margin logic |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue added | ₹38 Cr | ₹38 Cr |
| GM% | 11% | 11% |
| Conclusion | "Helps absorb Plant-B fixed cost" | **Programme contributes ₹4.2 Cr; required to break even on Plant-B fixed (₹6.8 Cr) — gap of ₹2.6 Cr** |

The programme is **necessary but not sufficient** to fix Plant-B's economics. Without 2 more such programmes, Plant-B remains structurally loss-making at the EBIT line.

This is exactly the kind of insight that distinguishes a CMA from a generalist analyst — the willingness to challenge a senior decision *with numbers*, respectfully.

## Insight 4 — 23 SKUs with negative contribution — but only 7 should be killed

Of the 23 negative-contribution SKUs:

| Action | SKUs | Annual ₹ at risk |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Drop now** (orphan, customer exited) | 7 | ₹0 (relief of ₹14 L cost) |
| **Re-price at next LTC** (mis-priced post-ABC) | 5 | ₹27 L upside if accepted |
| **Retain strategically** (basket-deal with Maruti) | 11 | ₹0 (already in basket pricing) |

**Net annual benefit: ₹41 L if execution clean.**

Recruiter angle: *"How would you sell SKU rationalisation to Sales?"* Answer: I wouldn't. I'd sell *7-SKU ratification* (the orphans) and *5-SKU re-pricing* — and explicitly defend the 11 strategic loss-leaders, because killing them would jeopardise ₹62 Cr of basket revenue with Maruti.

## Insight 5 — Power tariff is an unfunded ₹12 L/yr opportunity

Plant-A draws ~3.4 Mn kWh/yr at the MERC HT-I tariff (₹8.40/kWh + demand charge). It qualifies for **Open Access** (>1 MW load), where bilateral PPA rates are ~₹6.10-6.50/kWh. After accounting for cross-subsidy surcharge, wheeling charges, and SLDC fees, net savings ≈ ₹0.35-0.45/kWh.

| Consumption | kWh/yr | Net saving / kWh | Annual saving |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Plant-A | 3,400,000 | ₹0.40 | **₹13.6 L** |

A 9-month payback project, no capex (purely contractual). **Yet not on the operations roadmap.** Why? Because Power is owned by Plant Maintenance, not Finance — and Maintenance optimises uptime, not unit cost. Cross-functional gap.

---

## Insight 6 — The "non-controllable" 145 bps from RM is partially recoverable through clause renegotiation

Our LTC pass-through clause is:
- 80% of price change passed to customer
- 3-month lag
- Indexed to a published basket (60% HRC + 40% billet)

Comparator (Bharat Forge segment disclosure FY24): 95% pass-through, 2-month lag, basket includes scrap-credit adjustment.

If we matched Bharat Forge terms, ~85 of the 145 bps would have been recovered. The 60 bps gap is genuine insulation customers won't grant a Tier-2.

**Recommendation:** present the 60 bps gap to KAM as a target for the next LTC negotiation cycle (Aug-Oct 2026). **Not in our ₹2.18 Cr savings number** — it's a separate Sales workstream.

## Insight Summary Statement (the "elevator-pitch" line)

> *"The 460 bps margin compression is **42% controllable by Operations & Finance** (scrap, mix, SKU rationalisation, power, in-sourcing) — worth **₹2.18 Cr / yr** with average 7-month payback — and another **30%** is recoverable by **Sales** through stronger LTC pass-through clauses. The remaining ~28% is structural input-cost inflation that must be priced for, not absorbed."*

That single sentence is the entire project compressed. It's what goes in the executive summary, the LinkedIn post, the resume bullet, and the interview opener.
