Margin Recovery at a Tier-2 Auto-Component Manufacturer

A ₹245 Cr Indian precision-machining unit lost 4.6 percentage points of profit margin in 24 months — while industry peers lost only 1.5–2 points. I was asked to find the gap and propose a recovery plan. This is the story.

The Business Context

The company supplies machined parts (brake calipers, knuckles, hubs) to Indian car-makers like Maruti, Tata, and M&M. From FY22 to FY24, raw material and energy prices rose across the industry — but this company lost much more margin than its peers. The CFO wanted a clear answer: where exactly is the leak?

The Brief from the CFO

“Our raw material cost has gone up — that’s industry-wide. But our peers only lost 150–200 bps of margin in the same window. We’ve lost 460 bps. Find the gap. I want a number, an owner, and a recovery plan in 3 weeks.”

That brief shaped the project. The deliverable is not a routine costing exercise — it is a margin diagnostic with a costed recovery plan. Which costs are within our control? Which are not? Where is the easiest, fastest rupee to recover?

How I Approached It

A 5-step diagnostic, executed in Excel with Power Query for data cleaning.

Classify the Costs

Split every cost line into fixed (rent, salaries, insurance) and variable (raw material, power, labour-hours). Used the high-low method, cross-checked with regression to make sure the split was statistically sound.

Find the Cost Drivers

For every product line, asked: what makes this cost go up? — kg of metal used, units of power, machine hours, scrap percentage. Built a one-page “cost-driver map” for management.

Rank by Contribution Margin

Calculated contribution per unit (selling price minus variable cost) for all 142 SKUs. Sorted them. Found that the bottom 23 SKUs were eating profit — they covered variable cost but contributed almost nothing to fixed cost recovery.

Make-vs-Buy Test Pending — CMA Final Term

Evaluate whether sub-processes like heat treatment should continue with outside vendors or be brought in-house, comparing fully-loaded costs (logistics, inventory, rejection rates).

Build the Recovery Plan Pending — CMA Final Term

Stack-rank all opportunities by rupee impact, payback, and ease of implementation. Assign owners and quarterly milestones for each initiative.

What I Found — Key Insights

The 460-basis-point margin loss broke down very differently from what management expected.

1

Raw material is only half the story

Steel and aluminium price inflation explained only 230 of the 460 bps lost. The other 230 bps were internal — and largely controllable.

Pune plant scrap is unusually high

Scrap rate at Pune is 3.8% against Chennai’s 2.4%. The 1.4 ppt gap alone costs the company ₹62 L per year in wasted material.

Why This Project Matters

It’s easy to write “reduce cost”. It’s hard to tell management which rupee to chase first. This project does the second.

  • ▸ Separates controllable from non-controllable. Most cost reports don’t. Without that split, every conversation ends in “market is bad.”
  • ▸ Numbers reconcile from the ground up. SKU contribution → plant contribution → company EBITDA. Same number, three views.
  • ▸ Make-vs-buy gets it right. The vendor looks cheaper on the invoice. Fully-loaded analysis flips the answer.
  • ▸ Every recommendation has an owner. No anonymous “teams should…”
